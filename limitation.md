1. Machine Learning and NLP/LLM has a lot of overlap, it is hard to classify a job between them.
2. Many postings have requirements that match the category, but they are actually organizer position if look closely on the description.do 
3. Research assistant is scoped by the skills a posting wants, not by the department it sits in. A psychology or public-health lab that wants R, SPSS or data analysis counts; a chemistry lab that wants pipetting is Not relevant. Reason: relevance is defined as 1 - P(Not relevant), so anything in a real category surfaces as a match in the Week 8 shortlist and the app. Seniority is handled separately by JUNIOR_TITLE_PATTERN, so the category does not encode it.
4. A keyword can match a word without matching a role - the general form of point 2. The clearest case: the hint "lab" put 218 postings into Research assistant, of which 104 were SpaceX manufacturing roles ("Chemical Lab Technician", "EEE Parts Lab Specialist") and several were Anthropic describing itself as "the AI lab ecosystem". Fixed by replacing bare "lab" with research-scoped phrases. Before trusting any keyword count, check which companies it is pulling from - a term that looks like a skill can be one employer's house style.
5. The corpus is dominated by a few employers: SpaceX 1734 postings (26%), Databricks 565, Stripe 500, Anthropic 400. Every category's shape partly reflects how those companies write job ads rather than the job market. This is also why "spark" and "databricks" are deliberately absent from the Data engineering hints - 565 of the 590 "databricks" mentions are Databricks' own ads.
